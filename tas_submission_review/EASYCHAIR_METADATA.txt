Invalid Is Not Failure: Infrastructure Censoring Bias in Long-Horizon Agent Evaluation

ABSTRACT  (plain text; semantically identical to the compiled abstract)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Long-running agents sometimes stop without a valid task outcome because the
provider, runtime, or harness failed rather than the agent. Reported reliability
then depends on a reporting choice: score such runs as failures, or drop them.
We show these choices err in opposite directions, and that the error persists
even when infrastructure failures are independent of agent behaviour: a per-step
hazard compounds to an observation probability s^H in the horizon H, so
censoring becomes informative whenever H is associated with success. We give
assumption-free identification bounds, then show the complete-case estimand
satisfies p_cc - p = Cov(Y, s^H) / E[s^H], while scoring invalid runs as
failures can never overstate p. Two public corpora -- 80,036
software-engineering and 4,950 tool-agent trajectories -- behave exactly as the
theory predicts: where successful runs are much shorter, dropping invalid runs
inflates reliability by 2.7 points at a 2% hazard; where horizons are nearly
outcome-independent, that bias almost vanishes while invalid-as-failure still
costs 12.6 points. Censoring-aware weighting recovers the estimand when the
mechanism is known. We recommend benchmarks report validity counts and censoring
sensitivity rather than silently choosing a policy.
